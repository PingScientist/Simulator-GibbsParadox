Comparing Free Energies & Chemical Potentials

✓ Correct Formulation (With $1/N!$ factor)

Extensive & Physical

Because classical gas particles are microstates of identical field quanta, permutations over phase space are physically identical. We must divide by $N!$:

$$Z_{\text{gas}} = \frac{1}{N!} z_1^N = \frac{1}{N!} \left( \frac{V}{\lambda^3} q_{\text{gas, int}} \right)^N$$

Using Stirling's approximation $\ln N! \approx N \ln N - N$, the Helmholtz free energy is strictly extensive:

$$F = -k_B T N \left[ \ln\left( \frac{V}{N \lambda^3} q_{\text{gas, int}} \right) + 1 \right]$$

The chemical potential naturally depends on particle density $n = N/V = P / k_B T$:

$$\mu_{\text{gas}} = \left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial N}\right)_{T,V} = k_B T \ln\left( \frac{N \lambda^3}{V q_{\text{gas, int}}} \right) = k_B T \ln\left( \frac{P \lambda^3}{k_B T q_{\text{gas, int}}} \right)$$
Outcome: Equating $\mu_{\text{gas}} = \mu_{\text{surface}}$ yields the classical Langmuir formula: $$\theta = \frac{P}{P + P_L(T)}, \quad \text{where } P_L(T) = \frac{k_B T}{\lambda^3} \frac{q_{\text{gas, int}}}{q_{\text{internal}}} e^{-\epsilon_0 / k_B T}$$ Coverage $\theta$ depends exclusively on intensive pressure $P$. Doubling container volume $V$ at fixed $P$ changes nothing!

✗ Wrong Formulation (Distinguishable: No $1/N!$)

Gibbs' Paradox

If particles are treated as classically distinguishable entities with continuous trackable IDs, the product partition function lacks $1/N!$:

$$Z_{\text{gas}}^{\text{wrong}} = z_1^N = \left( \frac{V}{\lambda^3} q_{\text{gas, int}} \right)^N$$

The resulting free energy fails to be extensive ($F(2N, 2V) \ne 2F(N, V)$):

$$F^{\text{wrong}} = -N k_B T \ln\left( \frac{V}{\lambda^3} q_{\text{gas, int}} \right)$$

Differentiating with respect to $N$ leaves an expression with zero dependence on $N$ or $P$:

$$\mu_{\text{gas}}^{\text{wrong}} = -k_B T \ln\left( \frac{V}{\lambda^3} q_{\text{gas, int}} \right)$$
The Fatal Paradox: Equating $\mu_{\text{gas}}^{\text{wrong}} = \mu_{\text{surface}}$ yields: $$\frac{\theta}{1 - \theta} = \frac{\lambda^3 q_{\text{internal}}}{V q_{\text{gas, int}}} \implies \theta = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{V}{\lambda^3} \frac{q_{\text{gas, int}}}{q_{\text{internal}}}}$$ The coverage $\theta$ is independent of pressure $P$ (completely flat curve) and is falsely governed by the box volume $V$! Moving a gas into a larger container strips molecules from the surface even if pressure is unchanged.
